# GLOSSARY — GFSFUI

**General Framework of Structural Folding and Unfolding Intelligence**

This glossary defines the canonical terminology used across the GFSFUI repository.

The goal is consistency rather than exhaustive formalization.

---

## Brain Unit

A specialized intelligence component attached to a structurally justified region.

A Brain Unit may emerge when persistent local Delta justifies:

```text id="gloss01"
Persistent Delta
      ↓
Sub-CCC
      ↓
Branch
      ↓
Specialized Intelligence
      ↓
Brain Unit
```

A Brain Unit is therefore not merely a preallocated module.

Within GFSFUI, it can be interpreted as:

> **a structurally justified specialization created by persistent localized difference.**

---

## Candidate CCC

A provisional Cluster-Central CCC created from limited or newly observed evidence.

A Candidate CCC has not yet reached certified status.

It may contain:

* candidate Core,
* candidate DNA,
* context,
* supporting evidence,
* counter-evidence,
* confidence,
* and provenance.

---

## Candidate Delta

A newly observed structural difference that may explain a failure, subgroup, context change, or new behavior.

A Candidate Delta should generally be validated before becoming persistent structure.

---

## Candidate Equivalence

A provisional hypothesis that two local identities or structures are equivalent under a given structural perspective.

Candidate Equivalence is not the same as certified identity.

It should be tested through:

* structural comparison,
* Two-Way Search,
* context,
* outcome,
* and counter-evidence.

---

## Candidate Fold

A provisional structural hypothesis formed from limited evidence.

A Candidate Fold may contain:

* a Candidate CCC,
* candidate Core,
* candidate DNA,
* candidate Delta,
* or a candidate structural relation.

Canonical principle:

> **Few examples may be sufficient to initiate a Fold; they are not necessarily sufficient to certify it.**

---

## CallingGraph

A structural representation of program-call relationships.

A CallingGraph may contain:

* nodes,
* function or method calls,
* paths,
* entry points,
* control relations,
* failure paths,
* structural roles,
* context,
* and provenance.

Within GFSFUI, CallingGraphs are treated as Foldable individuals.

---

## CallingGraph Core

The reusable structural pattern shared by multiple CallingGraphs.

Example:

```text id="gloss02"
ENTRY
  ↓
VALIDATION
  ↓
AUTHORIZATION
  ↓
DOMAIN-SERVICE
  ↓
PERSISTENCE
```

A CallingGraph Core should preserve reusable structural organization without eliminating meaningful Delta.

---

## CallingGraph Delta

A structural difference relative to a CallingGraph Core.

Examples may include:

* cache layers,
* retry paths,
* asynchronous queues,
* audit calls,
* failure-handling branches,
* or context-specific services.

Conceptually:

$$
CG_i \approx CG_{Core} + \Delta_i
$$

---

## CallingGraph DNA

Reusable local structural relations extracted from CallingGraphs.

Common forms include:

* node-role combinations,
* Bigrams,
* Trigrams,
* path fragments,
* trigger structures,
* or role-normalized call relations.

Example:

```text id="gloss03"
VALIDATE → AUTHORIZE
AUTHORIZE → SERVICE
SERVICE → PERSIST
```

---

## Certification

The process by which a candidate structure becomes trusted enough for stronger future reuse.

Certification may include:

* search-based verification,
* counter-evidence analysis,
* statistical validation,
* simulation,
* tests,
* runtime execution,
* expert review,
* or domain-specific checks.

Certification is treated as an explicit maturity boundary.

---

## Certified CallingGraph

A CallingGraph that has passed an explicit validation or certification policy.

Certified CallingGraphs can become trusted members of future CallingGraph Folding populations.

---

## Certified Identity

A structural identity or equivalence that has passed sufficient evidence, counter-evidence, and validation checks.

A Certified Identity may later be promoted toward broader or Universal UTN.

---

## Cluster

A structural neighborhood of sufficiently similar individuals under a selected metric or structural perspective.

A cluster is not necessarily a permanent category.

It is a useful local region for:

* CCC construction,
* dispatch,
* search,
* and local intelligence.

---

## Cluster-Central CCC

A reusable structural center associated with a cluster or structural neighborhood.

A Cluster-Central CCC may combine:

* representative structure,
* probabilities,
* outcome distributions,
* dispatch information,
* DNA,
* context,
* Delta,
* and provenance.

It is more than a geometric centroid.

Within GFSFUI, it often acts as a joint among:

> **Representation + Probability + Dispatch + Validation**

---

## CCC

A reusable structural representation used for organization, probability, dispatch, triggering, validation, or other Structural Intelligence functions.

The exact expansion and implementation may vary across the broader research program.

Within GFSFUI, the important role is structural rather than purely statistical.

---

## Collective Structural Learning

A learning process in which reusable structural knowledge accumulates across individuals, runs, systems, or participants.

Collective Structural Learning may preserve:

* Certified CCCs,
* Candidate Folds,
* DNA,
* Delta,
* successful structures,
* failed structures,
* counter-evidence,
* identities,
* and provenance.

The goal is to reduce repeated rediscovery.

---

## Compute-by-Need

An architectural principle in which increasingly expensive intelligence is invoked only when cheaper structural mechanisms are insufficient.

Example escalation:

```text id="gloss04"
Index / Cache
     ↓
Metric Routing
     ↓
CCC
     ↓
DNA / Rules
     ↓
Small Specialist
     ↓
Large ANN / LLM
     ↓
External Tool / Human
```

---

## Context

The local conditions under which an identity, structure, CCC, DNA, Delta, or outcome should be interpreted.

Context may include:

* domain,
* environment,
* time,
* role,
* task,
* state,
* program,
* market regime,
* experimental setting,
* or target system.

Canonical principle:

> **Fold does not delete Context; it separates reusable Core from recoverable Context.**

---

## Context-Preserving Unification

A unification process that identifies shared structure without erasing meaningful local differences.

Conceptually:

$$
Identity_A = Core + Context_A + \Delta_A
$$

$$
Identity_B = Core + Context_B + \Delta_B
$$

The Core supports comparison.

Context and Delta preserve local meaning.

---

## Core

The reusable shared structure across a population, cluster, or family of individuals.

Core is what survives Structural Folding as common structure.

It should not be interpreted as meaning that all local differences are unimportant.

---

## Core ± Delta

A canonical GFSFUI representation of structural commonality and variation.

Conceptually:

$$
S_i \approx Core + \Delta_i
$$

Core captures reusable shared structure.

Delta captures meaningful deviation.

---

## Core-Preserved Generation

An Unfolding strategy that attempts to preserve certified Core structure while adapting only the required Delta for a new goal or context.

Example:

```text id="gloss05"
Certified Core
      +
Target Context
      +
Required Delta
      ↓
Candidate Structure
```

---

## Counter-Evidence

Structurally relevant evidence that contradicts or weakens a Candidate Fold, CCC, DNA relation, identity, or generated structure.

Counter-evidence is not treated merely as a negative score.

It may reveal:

* missing context,
* hidden subgroups,
* incorrect identities,
* inadequate metrics,
* or new Delta.

---

## Counter-Evidence Structural Search

Deliberate Structural Search for cases where a candidate structural relation fails.

Canonical question:

> **Where does the current Fold fail?**

---

## Delta

A meaningful structural difference relative to an existing Core, CCC, node, identity, or Fold.

Delta is one of the most important objects in GFSFUI because it connects:

* contradiction,
* specialization,
* local growth,
* and future Brain Units.

---

## Difference Localization

The process of assigning a meaningful structural difference to a specific location.

Example:

```text id="gloss06"
Domain
  ↓
Metric Perspective
  ↓
Tree
  ↓
Node
  ↓
CCC
  ↓
Delta
```

Difference Localization turns a vague exception into an addressable learning problem.

---

## Dispatch

The process of routing an individual, query, requirement, or observation toward a relevant structural region or intelligence component.

Dispatch may use:

* metric similarity,
* CCC,
* DNA,
* probability,
* tree structure,
* rules,
* or plugins.

---

## DNA

A compact combination of structurally meaningful elements used for:

* dispatch,
* triggering,
* comparison,
* search,
* verification,
* or identity discovery.

DNA may include:

* selected feature combinations,
* Bigram relations,
* Trigram relations,
* structural roles,
* or domain-specific elements.

It should not be interpreted as biological DNA unless explicitly used in a biological context.

---

## End-to-End Structural Traceability

The ability to trace a decision, Fold, CCC, DNA, Delta, identity, or generated structure back through the structural pipeline to its supporting sources.

Example:

```text id="gloss07"
Outcome
  ↓
Node Intelligence
  ↓
CCC / DNA
  ↓
Node
  ↓
Metric Perspective
  ↓
Starmap
  ↓
Original Individual
```

---

## Evidence Package

A structured collection of information used to evaluate a candidate structural object.

An Evidence Package may contain:

* supporting cases,
* counter-evidence,
* alternatives,
* context,
* metric perspective,
* validation results,
* confidence,
* provenance,
* and certification state.

---

## Fold

Short form for Structural Folding.

See **Structural Folding**.

---

## Fold-Guided UTN Evolution

A progressive process in which Local UTN enables Folding, and Structural Folding/Search then improves identity unification.

Canonical loop:

```text id="gloss08"
Local UTN
    ↓
Fold
    ↓
Structural Similarity
    ↓
Candidate Equivalence
    ↓
Two-Way Search
    ↓
Counter-Evidence
    ↓
Certified Unification
    ↓
Better UTN
    ↓
Better Fold
```

---

## Fold–Search–Unfold

A compact description of the central GFSFUI operational sequence:

```text id="gloss09"
Fold Experience
      ↓
Search Structural Memory
      ↓
Unfold Toward a Goal
```

Validation and Refolding normally follow.

---

## Fold–Unfold Intelligence

An intelligence architecture in which concrete experience is Folded into reusable structural memory and that memory is later Unfolded toward new goals or contexts.

Folding and Unfolding are complementary but not exact inverses.

---

## Folding Quality

The quality of a Fold as measured not only by compression but by future usefulness.

Possible criteria include:

* searchability,
* localization,
* Delta preservation,
* provenance,
* identity resolution,
* reusability,
* and future Unfoldability.

Canonical principle:

> **A useful Fold should support useful future Unfolding.**

---

## GenericContainerStarmap

The canonical structural representation interface used in GFSFUI.

It converts a domain-specific individual into a structurally operable representation.

A GenericContainerStarmap may contain:

* scalar features,
* categorical features,
* sequences,
* roles,
* relations,
* context,
* outcome,
* Bigram/Trigram features,
* graph properties,
* and domain-specific elements.

It defines the **Structuralization Boundary** between the domain-specific world and reusable Structural Intelligence machinery.

---

## Generic Per-Node Intelligence

The general mechanism by which different structural nodes can host different intelligence components.

Possible node intelligence includes:

* CCC,
* DNA,
* rules,
* ANN,
* LLM,
* domain algorithms,
* search policies,
* validation policies,
* plugins,
* or human review.

---

## Generic Structural Folding

The domain-general process of transforming structurally comparable individuals into reusable structural memory.

Canonical transformation:

> **Individuals → Comparable Representations → Similarity → Shared Structure → Difference Localization → Dispatch → Structural Memory**

---

## Generic Structural Representation

The process of converting domain-specific individuals into structurally comparable forms.

The canonical interface is GenericContainerStarmap.

---

## GFSFUI

**General Framework of Structural Folding and Unfolding Intelligence**

A general engineering and research framework connecting:

* structural representation,
* Folding,
* localization,
* Structural Search,
* counter-evidence,
* continual growth,
* Unfolding,
* identity,
* validation,
* certification,
* and Refolding.

Canonical lifecycle:

> **Represent → Fold → Localize → Search → Challenge → Unfold → Validate → Certify → Grow → Refold**

---

## Global Storage, Local Activation

A scaling principle in which large amounts of structural memory are retained globally while only a small relevant region is activated for a given task.

Related concept:

> **Large Structural Memory, Small Active Intelligence Footprint**

---

## Growth-by-Need

A principle in which the system grows structure only when existing structure no longer adequately explains persistent evidence.

Example:

```text id="gloss10"
Existing Structure Works
      ↓
Reinforce

Existing Structure Fails Persistently
      ↓
Delta
      ↓
Grow
```

---

## Identity

The persistent or structurally meaningful entity being resolved across observations, contexts, representations, or systems.

Identity is distinct from the name used to reference it.

Canonical principle:

> **Identity precedes Naming.**

---

## Identity Resolution

The process of determining whether a new observation or local representation belongs to an existing identity or requires a new identity.

---

## Identity Resolution under Partial Observation

Identity Resolution when only incomplete state, history, or context is available.

This is common in:

* perception,
* tracking,
* world models,
* software systems,
* and structural search.

---

## Intelligence Address

A structural location at which specialized intelligence can operate.

An address may include:

```text id="gloss11"
Domain
Metric Perspective
Tree
Node
CCC
Context
```

The concept supports local learning and Per-Node Intelligence.

---

## Local A/B Validation

A comparative validation process performed within a specific structural region.

Example:

* A = current node structure,
* B = candidate CCC / Delta / branch.

Local A/B reduces the scope of change and supports Low-Blast-Radius Learning.

---

## Local Identity

An identity valid within a bounded context, program, dataset, domain, or observation system.

Local Identity is a practical starting point before universal identity is available.

---

## Local Structural Growth

Structural growth confined to a localized region.

Possible forms include:

* probability updates,
* CCC refinement,
* DNA refinement,
* new Delta,
* new branches,
* or new Brain Units.

---

## Local UTN

A local Typing/Naming/Identity representation sufficient to support comparison and structural operations within a bounded context.

A useful Local UTN should be:

1. Comparable
2. Context-Preserving
3. Traceable
4. Promotable

---

## Localization

The process of reducing a large structural search or reasoning space to a smaller relevant region.

Localization may be performed by:

* indexes,
* metrics,
* tree dispatch,
* CCC,
* DNA,
* or other structural mechanisms.

Localization is a central operator across prediction, search, growth, and Unfolding.

---

## Low-Blast-Radius Learning

A learning objective in which changes are confined to the smallest structurally justified region.

Conceptually:

$$
\Delta S_{system} \approx \Delta S_{local}
$$

when the new evidence is structurally localized.

---

## Metric Differential Structure

A general structure that organizes shared structure and meaningful differences using metric or structural comparison.

Possible implementations include:

* trees,
* forests,
* DAGs,
* networks,
* or multiple perspective structures.

The fundamental operation is:

> **Similarity → Shared Structure → Difference Localization → Dispatch**

---

## Metric Differential Tree

A canonical tree-based implementation of Metric Differential Structure.

Nodes may contain:

* CCCs,
* DNA,
* Delta,
* probabilities,
* search policies,
* and local intelligence.

---

## Metric Perspective

One specific similarity, distance, or structural comparison view over a population.

Multiple Metric Perspectives can coexist.

Disagreement among them may expose hidden Delta.

---

## Minimum Sufficient UTN

The minimum identity layer required to begin useful Folding.

It must be:

* Comparable,
* Context-Preserving,
* Traceable,
* Promotable.

It does not need to be globally universal.

---

## Naming

An operational or linguistic handle attached to an identity.

Examples include:

* strings,
* IDs,
* keys,
* paths,
* URIs,
* labels,
* or symbolic names.

Naming should not be confused with the deeper Identity Resolution problem.

---

## Negative Structural Memory

Stored knowledge about failed, rejected, deprecated, contradictory, or unsafe structures.

Examples include:

* failed CallingGraphs,
* rejected Candidate Folds,
* known counter-evidence,
* rejected equivalences,
* or context-specific failures.

Negative Structural Memory reduces repeated rediscovery of known mistakes.

---

## Node

A structural location within a Metric Differential structure or related hierarchy.

A node may contain:

* CCC,
* DNA,
* Core,
* Delta,
* probabilities,
* search policies,
* evidence,
* and Per-Node Intelligence.

---

## Open Intelligence Architecture

An architecture in which multiple intelligence mechanisms can participate at different structural locations.

GFSFUI does not require one model type to dominate the system.

---

## Per-Metric Perspective Intelligence

An architecture in which different metrics or structural perspectives can organize and activate different local intelligence mechanisms.

---

## Per-Node Intelligence

Intelligence attached to a specific node or structural region.

The node determines where the intelligence applies.

Examples:

* CCC,
* rules,
* small ANN,
* LLM specialist,
* domain model,
* user plugin.

---

## Persistent Delta

A repeatedly observed and validated Delta that appears to represent stable structural difference rather than noise.

Persistent Delta may justify:

* new CCCs,
* branches,
* specialists,
* or Brain Units.

---

## Policy-Guided Structuralization

The process of using a policy to decide how raw domain objects are segmented, selected, represented, and converted into GenericContainerStarmaps.

Structuralization policy itself may be compared or learned through A/B evaluation.

---

## Progressive Structural Evidence Expansion

A search strategy that expands outward from local structural regions only as needed.

Example:

```text id="gloss12"
Same Node
   ↓
Neighbor Nodes
   ↓
Same Tree
   ↓
Other Metrics
   ↓
DNA / CCC
   ↓
Domain
   ↓
Cross-Domain
   ↓
External Evidence
```

---

## Progressive UTN Bootstrapping

The practical process of beginning with Local UTN and progressively developing broader identity systems through Folding, Search, and validation.

---

## Provenance

Information that links a structural abstraction back to its source.

Examples include:

* original individual,
* dataset,
* CallingGraph node,
* method,
* file,
* observation,
* run,
* or source location.

Provenance supports traceability, rollback, validation, and Refolding.

---

## Refold

To Fold validated new experience back into Structural Memory.

Refolding closes the learning loop.

---

## Refoldability

The property that an Unfolded result can be structurally represented, validated, and reintroduced into future Structural Folding.

Canonical principle:

> **A useful Unfold should be Refoldable.**

---

## Structural Address

A structural location used to identify where an individual, Delta, CCC, specialist, or learning event belongs.

Related term: **Intelligence Address**.

---

## Structural Certification

The process of validating and promoting a structural object into trusted memory.

The object may be:

* CCC,
* DNA,
* identity,
* CallingGraph,
* Delta,
* or other reusable structure.

---

## Structural DNA

See **DNA**.

Structural DNA emphasizes that the encoded combination represents reusable structural relations rather than isolated values.

---

## Structural Equivalence

A relationship in which two objects are sufficiently similar under a selected structural perspective for a specific task.

Structural Equivalence is not necessarily Exact Identity.

---

## Structural Falsification

The deliberate search for where an existing or candidate Fold fails.

Canonical question:

> **Where does my current Fold fail?**

The purpose is to discover:

* counter-evidence,
* missing context,
* hidden Delta,
* wrong metrics,
* or better structures.

---

## Structural Folding

The process of transforming many concrete individuals into reusable structural knowledge.

A Fold may preserve:

* Core,
* Delta,
* CCC,
* DNA,
* structural neighborhoods,
* context,
* provenance,
* and Per-Node Intelligence.

Structural Folding is:

> **intelligence-oriented structural compression**

rather than compression for its own sake.

---

## Structural Folding Gain

A conceptual measure of memory reduction or structural reuse.

One possible form is:

$$
G_f =
\frac{M_{raw}}
     {M_{folded}}
$$

This metric should not be interpreted as sufficient on its own.

A Fold that destroys useful Delta or provenance may have high compression but poor intelligence value.

---

## Structural Folding Ratio

A related conceptual measure:

$$
\rho_f =
\frac{M_{folded}}
     {M_{raw}}
$$

---

## Structural Growth

The creation or refinement of persistent structure in response to validated evidence.

Possible growth includes:

* CCC refinement,
* DNA refinement,
* Delta formation,
* branching,
* specialist creation,
* or Brain-Unit specialization.

---

## Structural Intelligence

Intelligence organized around explicit structure, structural comparison, dispatch, reusable memory, localized difference, and structural evolution.

Within GFSFUI, Structural Intelligence is not limited to trees or CCCs.

---

## Structural Learning Ladder

A canonical progression of increasing structural commitment:


* outcomes,
* context,

## Structural Search

Structural Search can include:

* outcome search,
* evidence search,
* identity search,

## Structuralization

## Structurally Escalated Intelligence

An architecture in which a problem moves through increasingly expensive intelligence stages only when earlier structural mechanisms are insufficient.

Related term: **Compute-by-Need**.

## Structural Unfolding

The process of using Folded structural memory, a goal, and context to construct a new concrete or semi-concrete structure.

Conceptually:

$$
StructuralMemory + Goal + Context
\rightarrow
CandidateStructure
$$

Unfolding is not exact reconstruction.

It is:

> **context-conditioned structural projection**

## Two-Phase Structural Search

A search architecture with:

### Phase 1

Cheap localization.

Examples:

* index,
* metric,
* tree,
* CCC.

### Phase 2

Deeper structural analysis.

Examples:

* DNA,
* Two-Way Search,
* graph search,
* ANN,
* LLM,
* external tools.

Canonical principle:

> **Cheap Localization → Deep Structural Search**

---

## Two-Way DNA CCC

A DNA-based structural relation examined in both directions.

Example:

$$
DNA \rightarrow Outcome
$$

and:

$$
Outcome \rightarrow DNA
$$

The goal is stronger structural consistency analysis, not automatic proof of causality.

---

## Two-Way Structural Search

Search that follows important structural relations in both directions.

Examples:

```text id="gloss17"
DNA ↔ Outcome

Individual ↔ CCC

Node ↔ DNA

CallingGraph ↔ Bigram

Identity ↔ Structural Role
```

---

## Unfold

Short form for Structural Unfolding.

See **Structural Unfolding**.

---

## Unfoldability

The degree to which Folded structural memory can support useful projection into new goals or contexts.

Unfoldability can be viewed as one criterion of Folding quality.

---

## Unfold-by-Need

A principle in which the system reuses existing certified Core and Unfolds only the Delta required by a new goal.

---

## Universal Identity

A broadly reusable identity that has survived sufficient cross-context structural comparison and validation.

Universal Identity should be treated as evidence-driven rather than assumed in advance.

---

## Universal Naming

A globally stable operational naming scheme associated with an identity.

Universal Naming is useful but conceptually distinct from Universal Identity.

---

## Universal UTN

A mature cross-context or cross-domain Typing/Naming/Identity layer.

Within GFSFUI, Universal UTN is an optional later-stage result of progressive identity evolution rather than a mandatory prerequisite for initial Folding.

---

## UTN

A broader Typing/Naming/Identity infrastructure used to make structural entities comparable and operationally addressable.

Within GFSFUI, UTN should be understood as supporting:

* Typing,
* Identity Resolution,
* Context-Preserving Unification,
* and Naming.

---

## Validation

The process of evaluating whether a candidate structure should be:

* reinforced,
* refined,
* split,
* merged,
* rejected,
* or certified.

Validation may be local or global depending on the scope of the structural change.

# Canonical Principles

### Structuralization

> **Represent before generalizing.**

### Folding

> **Fold repeated structure, but preserve meaningful Delta.**

### Localization

> **Use structure to determine where intelligence should act.**

### Search

> **Search structure, not only raw data.**

### Falsification

> **Search explicitly for where the current Fold fails.**

### Sparse Learning

> **Initiation is not certification.**

### Growth

> **Grow locally where persistent difference justifies it.**

### Identity

> **Identity precedes Naming.**

### UTN

> **Localize first. Universalize progressively.**

### Unfolding

> **Search before expensive Unfolding when reusable structural memory exists.**

### Certification

> **Validate before strong reuse.**

### Refolding

> **New validated experience should become future structural memory.**
